Video storyboard

Shot-by-shot plan for the six demo clips D1 to D6. Every clip is a screen recording of the live system (dc2-live on AWS, real GitHub Actions runs, a real benchmark run); editing removes waiting time and labels every removal with the real elapsed time. Commands and pass conditions live in the demo plan; this page fixes what is filmed, in which order, and how it is cut.

Shot conventions

Each shot has a time range in the final cut, a screen, an action, a burned-in caption, one voice-over sentence and an edit note. Text added in editing is either a caption or a time label, never a fabricated number.

Recording sessions

Two sittings. Session A records D1, D2, D3, D5 and D4 under one database warm-up. Session B records D6, which changes production and is mostly waiting for GitHub runners.

Both Aurora clusters pause after 300 s without connections and need a warm-up before the next database call succeeds (6 · Warm-up). Session A keeps them awake by never leaving more than 5 minutes between takes; every take of D1 to D4 touches both databases. If a gap runs longer, the warm-up is repeated before the next take.

Take naming

Raw takes are named D<clip>-<session>-T<take>-<yyyymmdd-hhmm>.mov, for example D4-A-T2-20261024-1512.mov. They stay in the private recording folder outside Git, together with a one-line log per take: start time in UTC, job or run identifiers, outcome, and keep or discard. A discarded take is kept until the final clips are checked. Final clips are D1.mp4 to D6.mp4 in site/clips/.

Editing and export

Cutting only removes waiting time; every removal is labelled with the real elapsed time, and every state change stays on screen long enough to read.

Tools

Any editor that can cut, change speed, freeze a frame and add text will do. On a Mac, screen recording ships with the system: QuickTime Player's New Screen Recording (or Shift-Command-5) records the full screen or a selected area. QuickTime can also trim a take. iMovie, if installed, covers the rest: split, speed change, freeze frame, titles, a voice-over track, and export. FFmpeg, already installed for the demo fixtures, can burn the time labels with its drawtext filter and convert the export if needed.

Order of work per clip

  1. Pick the take from the log and note the raw timecodes of every state change in the shot table.
  2. Cut to the shot table's ranges. Each removed or sped-up span gets its label: wait trimmed · real elapsed 7m16s or ×N · real elapsed 4m12s, where N is the real length divided by the kept length, rounded to a whole number.
  3. Add the captions and highlight boxes from the table.
  4. Record the voice-over lines against the cut, one take per line; lower the D1 playback sound under narration.
  5. Export 1920×1080, 30 fps, H.264 video, AAC audio, MP4 container, named D1.mp4 to D6.mp4, into site/clips/.
  6. Check each length with ffprobe (command in 6 · Recording preparation): each clip within 1 s of its row in the overview, total at most 460 s.

site/clips/ has a .gitignore that ignores everything but itself, and the publishing workflow excludes the folder, so the videos are never committed or published. The deck plays them from that folder on the presenting laptop; sharing them with the team needs a separate private copy.
