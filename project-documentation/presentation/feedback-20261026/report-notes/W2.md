## Draft: Platform Design

### Deriving the bounded contexts

A bounded context defines where terms and rules form one coherent model; it is not automatically a process, database or Lambda boundary. We use differences in vocabulary and required consistency to derive the two implemented business contexts. This is an interpretation of the implementation, not evidence of historical team negotiation. The method follows [Fowler's bounded-context explanation][DDD-BC] and [Evans's strategic and tactical patterns][DDD-REF].

| Business capability | Language and rule | Immediate consistency needed | Chosen context and evidence | Alternative assessed / current reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Reserve and adopt a source | UploadReservation, declared bytes, verified source pin, Asset | Finalize locks/rechecks reservation; writes pin + new Asset + submit outbox in one commit | **Media Management**: `direct_intake.reserve/finalize` [M-intake] | **Separate Upload context:** would split the existing adoption transaction and introduce a distributed handoff without a demonstrated separate model. It creates a new Asset with `source_asset_id` by identity. |

### Aggregate derivation and membership

Aggregate boundaries protect invariants rather than collect all related tables. References to another root do not make it a child. We follow [Vernon's identity-reference guidance][DDD-AGG] while explicitly showing the implementation's multi-record and occasional multi-root transactions. Composition in these figures means interpreted ownership of an entity or value, not SQL cascade behavior or a real SQLAlchemy relationship collection. Identity distinguishes domain entities from values: Attempt has its own identity and lifecycle; SourceVersion stores a pin under its owner Asset identity, so this review classifies it as a persisted value record rather than a separate domain entity. Source is the actual frozen contract value, and Output is a mutable DTO whose field data is stored in JSON. [M-upload] [P-model] [C-v1] [P-output]

### Messages and observed domain changes

The five message kinds are commands, internal work notifications and full state-transfer results. There are no persisted, separately named `AssetUploaded`/`JobStarted` event types and no event-sourced aggregate. `result` is emitted on accepted state changes, not only completion; heartbeat emits none. A repair can republish an existing queued-work message without another aggregate state change. [C-v1] [M-message] [P-emit] [P-life] [P-repair]

`contracts.v1.Envelope` declares the common types: `schema_version: int`, `message_id: str`, `kind: str`, `owner_id: str`, `asset_id: str`, `job_id: str | None`, `attempt_id: str | None`, `state_version: int`, `source: Source | None`, `payload: dict`. The contract declares payload as a generic dictionary; the kind-specific field definitions below come from **actual producers/consumers**, not five fictional typed contract classes. ### Ubiquitous-language corrections

- **Asset** includes uploaded and edit-derived videos; its ID is Media's public job ID. **Job** is the actual Processing ORM class, while **ProcessingJob** is a response DTO. [M-model] [M-edit] [M-public] [P-output]
- **Source pin** is the immutable six-field descriptor including S3 `version_id`. `Asset.source_revision` is a positive integer and is not S3 VersionId. A direct upload pin stays on UploadReservation; a copied edit pin is a SourceVersion value record. [M-model] [M-upload] [M-source] [C-v1]
- **Lease** is the Attempt token, expiry and deadline checked on the DB clock, conveyed locally through Claim. **Projection** is Media's validated local snapshot, not a shared Job object. [P-life] [P-claim] [M-project]

### Where our DDD is anaemic

Asset, User, UploadReservation, Job, Attempt and the ledger classes are SQLAlchemy records with field/constraint declarations. They do not encapsulate child collections or expose business methods. Owner checks, source adoption, retry/cancel decisions, lease fences and result validation live in repository/service module functions that query and mutate SQLAlchemy sessions directly. Consequently, **Asset** and **Job** aggregate roots in these diagrams are interpretations of consistency and lifecycle boundaries, not framework-enforced aggregate objects. The code also deliberately spans independent reservation/Asset or guard/Job records within local transactions. These are explicit exceptions to strict one-aggregate-per-transaction design, not evidence that the proposed roots have fully isolated consistency boundaries. Independent existence establishes a lifecycle difference; it does not by itself prove a correct aggregate boundary. Any future rich model must revisit those boundaries or justify the exceptions. [M-finalize] [P-cancel] Source and Actor are frozen primitive-field values. Claim and Envelope are shallow-frozen dataclasses: their nested dict/list payloads remain mutable. Output is a mutable Pydantic DTO, while operations/outputs are largely JSON data. The diagrams make the existing policies inspectable; they do not claim a richer OO implementation or perfect one-aggregate-per-transaction DDD discipline. [M-model] [M-upload] [P-model] [M-intake] [M-command] [P-command] [P-life] [C-v1]
