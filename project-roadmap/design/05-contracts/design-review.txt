RESOLVED — P1: cancellation after an uncertain/in-flight SubmitJob.
Evidence:
  processing.openapi.json: paths["/assets/{asset_id}/job"].delete;
    paths["/jobs"].post.description
  persistence-model.json: processing_db.submission_guards,
    transactions[1] (Submit processing), transactions[5] (Delete),
    retention entry "Request keys and tombstones"
  content.json: internal-contracts, persistence, transactions, data-inventory
  contract-cases.json: CT-24
MM can cancel by stable asset_id without knowing the internal job UUID.
Submit and cancellation serialize on the same owner-bound persistent guard.
Cancel-first reserves a deleted guard and later Submit returns 410;
Submit-first cancellation tombstones the job, cancels attempts and schedules
output cleanup. Another owner receives 404. Guard retention lasts for the
entire demo environment. This resolves the lost-response and delayed-submit
barrier without cross-database access or circular authorization.

The public edit contract explicitly commits immutable operations with the

Reconciliation replays those saved operations, preserving the Processing
fingerprint through MM restart or Processing outage. The independent source
copy remains durable and does not depend on the parent deletion lifecycle.

compatible with this distinction; synchronous probing is not required.

Nonblocking wording precision:

    paths["/assets/{asset_id}/job"].delete.responses["204"].description
This sentence promises a cleanup intent even when no job exists, while
processing_db.cleanup_intents is keyed by job_id. The operation description
already distinguishes cancel-first guard creation from submit-first output
cleanup, so the intended behavior is clear and the cancellation barrier is
sound. Suggested wording: "Owner-bound cancellation guard committed; job
cancellation and output cleanup intent committed when a job exists."
No new cleanup table or placeholder job is needed.
